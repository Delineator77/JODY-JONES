The Method

What is a Gauntlet Loop?

A prompting method by Matt Shumer: give an agent a bar it can't talk its way around, let it split the work, and never let the builder grade itself. GAUNTLET was built exactly this way — the steps below are the loop, not a summary of it.

01 — GOAL

The destination, not the route

The goal: a modern, AAA-quality first-person shooter in Three.js, zero external assets. No architecture prescribed — the agent chose how.

02 — THE BAR

A bar it can't argue with

A concrete AAA arena-shooter rubric (Call of Duty / DOOM / Destiny). The reference wins by default; "pretty good for AI" is not a passing grade.

03 — SPLIT

Smallest pieces that can be judged

Lighting, environment art, enemy design, weapon & gunplay, HUD & juice — each improved and judged on its own.

04 — BLIND CRITICS

Never let the builder grade itself

Each piece is judged by a fresh, blind critic in its own clean context that renders the actual pixels itself and reports the single biggest gap — no builder's rationale allowed.

05 — LOOP

Keep going

Builder fixes the biggest gap → re-render → critic again → repeat. Two full waves of five-then-three blind critics ran on GAUNTLET.

06 — STOP, DON'T FINISH

The bar stays out of reach

You stop the run; it never "finishes." With a high enough bar there is always one more gap to close.

The Gauntlet in Action

What the blind critics changed

Each critic rendered the game, looked at the real pixels, and named the biggest reason a real AAA shooter would beat it. Two examples of before → after.

Atmosphere & environment
Baseline a black-crushed void

Every surface crushed to pure black; empty sky up top; cover read as featureless blobs.

After a built, lit space

Opposing fill light, luminous haze, an overhead truss, a distant skyline, glowing edge-lit cover, floating dust.

Enemy readability
Baseline a floating spark

The enemy's body was the same color as the crates — you tracked a pinpoint glow, not a creature.

After a readable threat

A glowing rim outline, lifted albedo, a menacing hunched silhouette, animated legs and a menace telegraph.

Bestiary

Three ways to die

Round 1 made every enemy glow. Round 2's critic caught that they all glowed the same — so the archetypes were redesigned to differ by silhouette, not just hue.

Drone
Fast · fragile · swarms

A lean, forward-hunched carapace that closes the distance and lunges. Cheap alone, lethal in numbers.

Spitter
Ranged · keeps its distance

A bulbous acid gland marks it at a glance. Lobs glowing orbs from range while you're busy up close.

Brute
Slow · armored · heavy

Broad armored shoulder plates and a heavy low stance. Soaks damage and hits like a truck.

Play It

Survive the arena

Escalating waves of hostiles in a neon arena. Clearing a wave refills your magazine and patches your armor; headshots on the glowing core deal double. It's one self-contained web page — no build step.
